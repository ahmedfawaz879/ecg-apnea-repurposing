# Mechanism: Why an ECG Carries Respiratory Information at All

An obstructive apnea event collapses the airway; the resulting hypoxia
and rising CO2 trigger a brief cortical/autonomic arousal to resume
breathing. That arousal produces a stereotyped autonomic signature: heart
rate typically **slows during the apneic phase** (vagally mediated) and
**surges on resumption of airflow** (sympathetic activation at arousal) —
a repeating pattern called **cyclic variation of heart rate (CVHR)**.
Because heart rate is entirely visible in the R-R interval series of an
ordinary single-lead ECG, this respiratory-driven autonomic pattern is
recoverable from a sensor that was never built to sense breathing at
all. This is the entire reason the modeling in this repository can work
in principle — stated up front rather than treated as an unexplained
empirical curiosity.
